Lace math

The lace snaps on a Monday morning, and the replacement rack is a wall of centimeter numbers with no logic on it. Lace math counts the eyelets, walks the criss-cross, and tells you the length your shoes actually need - then picks the standard size to buy. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Lace length - eyelet pairs, the gap across and between rows, and tie allowance become the criss-cross length.
  • Size pick - any measured length rounds up to a labeled standard lace size, with the spare counted.
  • Quick pick - an EU shoe size becomes a labeled eyelet-pair estimate and a lace to grab off the rack.

Labeled norms

The model: criss-cross lacing is the bottom rung plus two diagonals per extra row, plus the tie allowance at each end. Labeled illustrative typicals: about 4.5 cm between eyelet columns, about 2.5 cm between rows, and 15 cm per end to tie. Labeled standard lace sizes: 60, 75, 90, 100, 120, 140, 160, 180, 200 cm - always round up. Labeled illustrative norm for the quick pick: about 4 to 8 eyelet pairs across EU sizes. These are reference values, not rules - measure your own shoe when it matters.
